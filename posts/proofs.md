# Thoughts on Proving False Statements: 14 Proof Techniques I Used to Get 2 Bachelor's Degrees


This comedic post is inspired by [MIT's Top 10 Proof Techniques Not Allowed](https://mitadmissions.org/blogs/entry/mathematics-for-computer-science-top-10-proof-techniques-not-allowed/), which has given me many a giggles during particularly treacherous study sessions for exams. After I received the graded exams, I realized that I have used/seen similar proof techniques.

1. **Proof by Circles and Stars.** Write a bunch of loosely related jargon on the board, circle some things that seem important, star some things that seem relavant. Bonus points if everything but the circle is illegible.
2. **Proof by Coincidence.** If it sounds like numerology. 
3. **Proof by Denial of Wrongdoing.** A common occurance in regrade requests. 
4. **Proof by Reverse Engineering.** Particularly useful on an exam, when the ending statement is given and the assumptions are given, but the transitions in between are handwaving scribbling. 
5. **Proof by Attendance.** Obviously true, because I was there in class when it was stated. 
6. **Proof by Divine Vision, Gut Feeling, or Oddly Specific Dream.** I got this a lot when I realized that the only way I would pass an exam is by osmosis, so I would sleep using my textbook as a pillow. 
7. **Proof by Hope of Proof.** Assingment due at 11:59, proof attempted at 11:39. 
8. **Proof by Incomplete and Incorrect Example.** Combine with 3 to get good grades. 
9.  **Proof by Carefully Constructed Example.** Ask your favorite LLM 'Can you explain why this is true with an example?'
10. **Proof by Quantifier Overload.** Assume there exists a proof that x = y for all x and for all y. 
11. **Proof by 'Saved By The Bell'.** State the theorem 1 minute before the class ends. Say that the proof is straightfoward, and doesn't need to be explained in the interest of time. 
12. **Proof by Extreme Pacing.** Professors use this technique by speaking or delivering a lecture extremely slowly or extremely fast. Extremely fast pacing is perfect when testing students on understanding the proof, since otherwise some may have been able to understand. 
13. **Proof by Reference to Uncommon Corequisite.** 'You should have learned this in MATH 789.' Even better if the class hasn't been offered in years, or time conflicts with the course. 
14. **Proof by Apology.** On your exam, write 'Sorry, I don't know how to solve this problem, but can I have pity points for my pathetic attempt?'. Has worked for be surprisingly more often than expected!
